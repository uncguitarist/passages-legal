Curio Privacy Policy

Curio is a reading app for children. This page says what it collects, what it does not, and how to have it deleted.

Curio does not show advertising, does not track anyone across apps or websites, and contains no analytics or advertising software from any other company.

We never collect these

  • No names, except in emails you ask for. A reader's name is typed on the device and stays there. If a parent asks for reading reports by email, the report is put together on the device and includes the reader's name as typed, so it passes through on its way to the parent; it is not kept.
  • No free text. Apart from the email address a parent chooses to give for reports, the only information that can be sent is drawn from a fixed list of numbers and labels, and anything outside that list is refused rather than stored.
  • No contact details other than a parent's email for reports, no location, no photos, no microphone, no contacts. Dictation, where a child uses it, is handled by Apple's own keyboard and never reaches us.
  • No reading content. We record that a passage was read, not the passage.
  • No device model, operating system, or language.
  • No advertising identifier and no tracking of any kind.

What we do collect

A random install identifier

A 64-character random value created once when the app is installed. It is not your device's serial number and not Apple's advertising identifier, and it does not survive deleting the app.

A scrambled reader identifier

Each reader on the device gets a scrambled identifier, made by mixing their local profile id with a random value that never leaves the device. It lets one reader's results be grouped together without us holding anything that names them.

What happened in the app

Which screens were opened, which passages were started and finished, which answers were right or wrong, and which rewards were earned. Numbers and fixed labels only.

Problem reports

When something fails — a crash, a file that would not load, a setting that could not be read — the app reports the kind of failure and where in the code it happened. No file paths and no reading content.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, freezes or writes too much to storage, Apple's report of it is reduced on the device to the kind of problem and where in the code it happened before it is sent. Nothing a reader did or typed is in it.

A parent's email address, only if you ask for reports

If a parent asks for reading reports by email, the address they type is kept with the install's random identifier so the reports can be sent. Nothing is sent to it until the parent taps the confirm link in the first email.

Curio sends this information to one place: a database we run ourselves. No other company receives any of it, and it is never combined with information from any other app or website. The one exception is report emails a parent asks for, described below.

Reading reports by email

Only if a parent asks for them, from the grown-up part of the app. What is sent: the address they typed, how often they want reports, and their time zone. Each report is put together on the device (the reader's name as typed, story titles, minutes read, questions right and skills practised) and passes through on its way out; we do not keep it. A weekly school reading log waiting for a parent's approval is kept until they approve it, and never longer than 14 days.

Emails are sent through Google's Gmail service, from the app's own account. They have no tracking pixels and no tracked links.

Every email has a link to stop them. “Stop emails and remove my address” in the app deletes the address straight away; after the stop link, it is deleted within 30 days.

iCloud backup

Each reader's progress is backed up to the iCloud account the iPad is signed in to, so it can be brought back on a new iPad or after reinstalling. It is stored in that account's private iCloud storage, which Apple keeps private to the account; we cannot see it. Deleting a reader deletes their backup.

How long it is kept

Collected information is deleted automatically after 90 days. Weekly summaries of a reader's progress are kept for longer so a parent can see change over time; they contain only totals, never any of the detail above.

Deleting a reader's information

Deleting a reader's profile in the app removes everything stored on the device for that reader. To have anything already sent deleted as well, write to us at the address below with the date you deleted the profile and we will remove it.

Deleting the app removes all local information. The random value used to scramble reader identifiers is deleted with it, so nothing sent earlier can be connected to a later install.

Children

Curio is built to Apple's Kids Category rules. There is no sign-in, no account, no chat, no user-to-user contact, and no link out of the app that a child can reach. Purchases, settings and anything parent-facing sit behind a check only an adult can pass.

Contact

Questions, deletion requests, or anything that looks wrong: curio.reading@gmail.com.
